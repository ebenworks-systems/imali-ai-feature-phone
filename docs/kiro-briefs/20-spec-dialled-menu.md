You are working in this repository, the Imali AI feature-phone ledger. Read the steering files in .kiro/steering/ and the two finished specs in .kiro/specs/ledger-core/ and .kiro/specs/text-messages/, and the code in src/core and src/channels/sms, before you start.

Write a new spec, .kiro/specs/dialled-menu/, with requirements.md (numbered requirements in EARS form, "WHEN ... THE SYSTEM SHALL ..."), design.md (modules, public signatures, the screen flow and the exact screen texts) and tasks.md (small checkbox tasks, each naming its files and the requirements it meets, tests next to the code they test). Do not write any application code in this run: only the three spec files.

What the dialled menu must do (Africa's Talking USSD):

1. The phone network calls us with form fields sessionId, serviceCode, phoneNumber and text. `text` is everything the owner has typed in this session, joined by `*` (empty on the first request; "1*45*1" after choosing 1, typing 45, choosing 1). We answer `text/plain` starting `CON ` (session continues) or `END ` (session ends). The screen text after the prefix is at most 182 characters, plain ASCII, and fits on a small screen: short lines, numbered options.
2. The menu is stateless: every screen is computed from the whole `text` path plus the shop's records, by reading the inputs one by one through a small state machine. `0` goes back one screen and `00` goes to the main menu from any screen, so the machine must replay the path with those two inputs applied. An unexpected input on a choice screen shows the same screen again with "Choose a number from the list." as its first line, and never ends the session.
3. A phone number with no shop gets a short sign-up: "Welcome to the Imali AI ledger. Your shop name?" then a currency choice (1 R South Africa, 2 $ US dollar, 3 KSh Kenya, 4 N Nigeria, 5 GHS Ghana, 6 K Zambia, 7 P Botswana), then the shop is made and the main menu shows. Use createShop and validateShopName from src/core.
4. The main menu is the one in .kiro/steering/screens.md: the shop name, "Today: sales R1,240", and options 1 Sale, 2 Expense, 3 Owes (credit), 4 Paid back, 5 Today, 6 Who owes, 7 Undo last, 8 Close day. Option numbers never change.
5. Sale: amount, then "What was sold? 1 to skip", then a confirm screen that repeats the amount and item ("Save sale R45 bread? 1 Yes 2 No"). Expense: amount, then what it was for (1 to skip), then confirm. Owes: customer name, amount, item (1 to skip), confirm. Paid back: a numbered list of the customers who owe, largest first ("1 Tendai R50"), with "9 More" when the list does not fit and "8 Other name" to type a name, then amount, then confirm. Saving uses recordSale, recordExpense, recordDebt and recordPayment from src/core and ends the session with what was saved and the new figure, for example "END Saved: sale R45 bread. Sales today R1,285."
6. Amounts in the menu are read with parseAmount from src/core in the shop's currency. In the menu's amount field there is no quantity, so a space or comma followed by three digits is a thousands separator ("2 200" is R2,200). Zero, a negative number or words show the amount screen again with a plain reason. An amount above 1,000,000.00 gets a second confirm screen ("That is a big amount: R1,500,000. 1 Yes, save 2 No") before it is saved.
7. Today: END screen with sales total and count, spent, and money in hand. Who owes: the list largest first with the total owed, "9 More" for the next page, END when the owner leaves. Undo last: shows the last record that can be undone and asks "Undo sale R45 bread? 1 Yes 2 No"; Yes calls undoLastRecord and ends with "Undone: ..." and the new figure; "Nothing to undo." when there is none. Close day: END screen with the closing summary from getDayCloseSummary (sales and count, spent, in hand, credit given, paid back, total owed), fitted into 182 characters.
8. A saving request repeated by the network for the same sessionId must not save twice. Design how (for example a record id derived from the sessionId and the step, refused as a duplicate by the store), and test it.
9. The menu shares the ledger, the store and the money rules with the text messages, so a sale saved by text shows in the menu's today figure and the reverse.
10. Every screen is tested: a test helper drives a whole session step by step as a phone would (send "", then "1", then "1*45", ...) against the in-memory store, and every screen in every test is checked for the `CON `/`END ` prefix, ASCII and the 182-character limit, with the longest realistic data (a 24-character shop name, a 20-character customer name, amounts of R999,999.99). Add a `check:screens` npm script (replace the placeholder in package.json) that runs a script rendering every menu screen and every text reply with that longest data and fails if any is over its limit or not ASCII.

Keep the public entry point small: `handleUssd({ sessionId, serviceCode, phoneNumber, text, now, store }): Promise<{ response: string }>` in src/channels/ussd/, exported from src/channels/index.ts. No new dependencies. End with a short summary of the spec: the number of requirements and tasks.
